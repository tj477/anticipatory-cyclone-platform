# **Architecture Blueprint for AI-Driven Anticipatory Cyclone Risk and Vulnerability Modeling**

The paradigm of disaster management in regions hyper-vulnerable to extreme weather, particularly the Bay of Bengal and the broader Asia-Pacific (APAC) theater, is undergoing a profound structural shift. Historically, institutional responses have been fundamentally reactive, focusing on post-landfall search, rescue, and recovery operations. Extensive economic analyses and humanitarian evaluations repeatedly demonstrate that reactive frameworks are deeply inefficient, resulting in compounded human casualties, catastrophic infrastructure degradation, and prolonged economic paralysis. The strategic imperative has therefore transitioned toward pre-landfall anticipatory action. Rapid anticipatory action—executed three to five days prior to a cyclone's landfall—enables proactive evacuation, infrastructure hardening, and the immediate dispersal of parametric insurance liquidity to vulnerable populations1.  
Evaluations of anticipatory action frameworks, such as those piloted by the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UN OCHA) and the World Food Programme (WFP) in Bangladesh and Nepal, provide incontrovertible evidence of this model's efficacy. Empirical data reveals that delivering cash transfers just days before a flood or cyclone peaks generates profound socioeconomic benefits compared to equivalent transfers delivered after the event3. In these trials, households assigned to forecast-based anticipatory action (FbAA) exhibited a Food Consumption Score (FCS) 1.1 points higher and a Reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI) 1.1 points lower than those receiving post-disaster aid, indicating a significant reduction in reliance on negative coping mechanisms such as liquidating critical assets4. Furthermore, achieving these impacts through anticipatory action required substantially less capital; in the studied cohorts, the anticipatory program achieved comparable long-term welfare stability with a transfer size representing only a fraction of traditional post-disaster outlays4.  
To scale this proven humanitarian concept, decision-makers require real-time, hyper-local, and highly accurate predictive modeling. This document outlines a comprehensive architectural blueprint for a scalable, AI-powered predictive risk and vulnerability modeling platform. By fusing Google Earth Engine (GEE) satellite telemetry, advanced Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for simulating storm surge and local rainfall damage pathways, OpenStreetMap (OSM) infrastructure exposure mapping, and the multimodal reasoning capabilities of Gemini 3.7 Flash, this platform establishes a next-generation early warning and action execution system. Furthermore, it integrates automated Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) dispatches and blockchain-based smart contracts for instantaneous financial liquidity, serving as a holistic minimum viable product (MVP) for modern disaster mitigation.

## **High-Level System Architecture and Cloud Topology**

The proposed platform utilizes a decoupled, microservices-based architecture orchestrated entirely on cloud infrastructure, specifically leveraging Google Cloud Platform (GCP) services such as Cloud Run and Vertex AI6. This design paradigm ensures that individual computational workloads—ranging from processing heavy satellite imagery to running inference on deep learning models and parsing vector maps—can scale independently during a rapidly evolving meteorological crisis. A monolithic architecture would inevitably bottleneck during the massive parallel processing required to simulate regional hydrodynamics. Conversely, deploying asynchronous microservices allows the platform to maintain low-latency inference, which is critical when the window for anticipatory action is strictly confined to the 72 to 120 hours prior to landfall2.  
The architectural topology is structured across five distinct operational tiers, each executing highly specialized tasks before routing structured outputs to the central cognitive orchestrator.

| Architectural Tier | Primary Technologies & Protocols | Core Functionality |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Data Ingestion & Earth Observation** | Google Earth Engine (GEE), Sentinel-1 SAR, IMERG, Copernicus DEM GLO-30 | Aggregates pre-event baselines and real-time precipitation, wind, and radar backscatter data. |
| **Predictive Hydrodynamics** | Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), DUALFloodGNN | Generates rapid, low-latency simulations of storm surge height, pluvial damage pathways, and mass conservation. |
| **Exposure & Vulnerability Mapping** | OpenStreetMap (OSM) Overpass API, Overpass QL, GeoPandas | Extracts critical infrastructure vectors (power grids, arterial roads, medical shelters) within the predicted inundation zones. |
| **Cognitive Orchestration** | Gemini 3.7 Flash, Vertex AI, Python | Acts as the central reasoning agent, analyzing multimodal inputs (maps, tabular data) to structure risk assessments and trigger downstream actions. |
| **Dissemination & Liquidity** | CAP v1.2, LoRaWAN Mesh, Chainlink DONs, Solidity Smart Contracts | Dispatches localized, standard-compliant warnings and autonomously triggers parametric insurance cash transfers. |

## **Geospatial and Meteorological Data Ingestion Pipeline**

The foundation of any predictive risk platform is the continuous, automated ingestion of high-fidelity environmental telemetry. The platform utilizes Google Earth Engine via its Python API (geemap and ee) to programmatically access petabytes of satellite imagery without requiring local storage overhead, executing complex spatial reductions directly on Google's infrastructure8.

### **Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Flood Detection**

During severe cyclone events, extreme cloud cover renders traditional optical satellite imagery (such as Sentinel-2 or Landsat) virtually useless for real-time observation. Therefore, the platform relies on the Copernicus Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Ground Range Detected (GRD) dataset10. SAR operates in the C-band microwave frequency, seamlessly penetrating heavy cloud cover and intense precipitation to provide uninterrupted all-weather, day-and-night observation of the Earth's surface12.  
To dynamically detect and predict flood pathways, the platform utilizes a robust change-detection algorithm comparing pre-landfall (baseline) and synoptic backscatter values13. The radar backscatter coefficient (![][image1]) is analyzed primarily in the VV (vertical transmit, vertical receive) and VH (vertical transmit, horizontal receive) polarizations. Smooth water surfaces typically act as specular reflectors, scattering the radar signal away from the sensor, which results in distinctly low backscatter, appearing as dark pixels in the imagery13.  
The algorithmic workflow for flood detection executed within the GEE environment involves a multi-step refinement process to isolate genuine floodwater from radar anomalies. First, a speckle filter is applied, utilizing a focal median smoothing kernel (typically with a 100-meter radius) to reduce the inherent granular noise characteristic of SAR imagery14. Next, the system computes the logarithmic difference between post-event and pre-event composites, defined mathematically as ![][image2]13. Because SAR flood mapping can be highly sensitive to orbit geometry, the script processes ascending and descending orbits separately, subsequently fusing the per-orbit probabilities using a maximum operator to significantly reduce geometry-driven false negatives13.  
Following the change calculation, an automated Otsu thresholding mechanism is implemented over the ![][image3] histogram. This statistical technique dynamically identifies the optimal decibel (dB) cutoff for classifying "new water" without requiring manual human tuning, which is vital for an automated MVP13. To eliminate false positives—such as radar shadow in steep mountainous terrain or smooth concrete surfaces in urban areas mimicking dark water—the platform applies stringent plausibility masking. Using the Copernicus DEM GLO-30, slopes greater than a configurable threshold (e.g., 5 degrees) are masked out11. Furthermore, a Height Above Nearest Drainage (HAND) mask is applied, restricting flood probability to low-lying terrain near established drainage networks, typically capped at 40 meters13. Finally, the Joint Research Centre (JRC) Global Surface Water (GSW) dataset is integrated to exclude permanent water bodies, ensuring that the model isolates only anomalous, cyclone-driven inundation10.

### **Real-Time Hydrometeorological Forcing**

To complement the radar data and provide the predictive models with atmospheric context, real-time meteorological forcing data is ingested. This includes precipitation accumulation rates from the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) IMERG dataset, and wind speed, direction, and vorticity metrics from atmospheric models such as the ECMWF IFS13. This synthesis of historical SAR baselines, real-time radar changes, and live meteorological data provides the dense foundational tensors required for the predictive hydrodynamic modeling layer.

## **Advanced Hydrodynamic Modeling: Storm Surge and Pluvial Pathways**

Current state-of-the-art disaster response systems heavily rely on traditional numerical hydrodynamic solvers such as ADCIRC (Advanced CIRCulation) or SLOSH (Sea, Lake, and Overland Surges from Hurricanes)16. ADCIRC models solve shallow-water equations using highly detailed unstructured grids to provide exceptionally high-fidelity surge predictions, factoring in astronomical tides, wind drag, and atmospheric pressure variations17. However, these numerical models exhibit a critical, often fatal flaw in the context of rapid anticipatory action: profound computational latency. Simulating a high-resolution regional storm surge via ADCIRC can take hours or even days when running on traditional high-performance computing (HPC) clusters16. When the operational window for anticipatory action is strictly 72 to 120 hours prior to a cyclone's landfall, sacrificing 24 hours simply to wait for simulation computation is unacceptable, as it directly reduces the time available for physical evacuation and capital deployment2.

### **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**

To advance beyond current technological constraints, the architecture replaces the real-time execution of numerical solvers with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs)19. PINNs are trained offline using thousands of historical and synthetic ADCIRC and SLOSH simulations, learning the complex nonlinear dynamics of the ocean-atmosphere interface16. During this rigorous training phase, the neural network learns to map multidimensional meteorological inputs (such as central pressure, translation speed, wind drag coefficients, and tidal harmonics) directly to high-dimensional water surface elevation outputs across the coastal topography21.  
A standard data-driven machine learning approach acts as a "black box," optimizing solely for statistical fit. While fast, purely data-driven models risk generating highly unphysical results, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios that are increasingly common during unprecedented extreme weather events23. To fundamentally mitigate this risk, PINNs embed physical conservation laws directly into the learning process and the loss function25.

### **Simulating Local Rainfall Damage Pathways via DUALFloodGNN**

While coastal storm surges cause massive immediate devastation, cyclones also dump extreme volumes of precipitation inland, leading to severe pluvial (rainfall-driven) and fluvial (riverine) flooding. Predicting these local rainfall damage pathways requires routing water through complex urban and rural catchments. To achieve this, the platform integrates Graph Neural Network (GNN) architectures specifically designed for hydrology, such as DUALFloodGNN25.  
DUALFloodGNN formulates the coastal watershed as a directed graph ![][image6]. In this architecture, the nodes ![][image7] represent specific geographic regions or catchments containing a specific water volume, while the edges ![][image8] represent the physical flow pathways between these regions25. The model explicitly mimics the physical co-dependency of volume and flow: changes in water volume at a node are driven by flows along the edges, and flows are driven by the difference in water states between nodes25.  
The ![][image9] component of the loss function heavily penalizes the model if it predicts a sudden increase in water at a given node without predicting a corresponding inflow from neighboring edges. Enforcing this local and global conservation of mass prevents the accumulation of numerical errors during autoregressive "rollout" predictions, where the output at time ![][image10] is used as the input for time ![][image11]25. By deploying these PINN and GNN models on Vertex AI, the platform achieves zero-shot, real-time inference, generating high-resolution spatial surge maps and pluvial damage pathways in mere seconds. This retains the physical validity of a traditional solver while drastically expanding the operational window for municipal evacuation planning16.

## **Infrastructure Exposure and Vulnerability Mapping (OpenStreetMap)**

Anticipating the physical extent, depth, and velocity of the water is only the first half of predictive risk modeling; the second half is understanding the exact socioeconomic and infrastructural vulnerabilities trapped within that geographical extent. The platform systematically maps exposure for critical infrastructure—specifically power grids, arterial roads, and medical shelters—by interfacing dynamically with the OpenStreetMap (OSM) Overpass API27.

### **Overpass QL and Infrastructure Extraction**

The Overpass API allows the system to execute highly targeted, read-only Overpass Query Language (QL) requests to extract specific nodes, ways, and relations within the geographical bounding box of the PINN-predicted cyclone impact zone28. OpenStreetMap represents a uniquely valuable data source in regions like the Bay of Bengal, where proprietary municipal GIS data is often siloed, outdated, or entirely unavailable30.  
To isolate critical infrastructure necessary for disaster survival and recovery, the platform executes specific tag-based queries. The following table illustrates the core Overpass QL structures utilized by the platform's mapping microservice:

| Infrastructure Category | Overpass QL Tag Filter | OSM Element Type | Rationale for Extraction |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Electrical Grid** | \["power"="transformer"\] | Node / Area | Identifies substations and transformers susceptible to short-circuiting during storm surges, aiding in preemptive power de-energization. |
| **Arterial Evacuation Routes** | \["highway"\~"^(motorway|trunk|primary)"\] | Way | Isolates major connective roadways required for mass civilian evacuation and post-landfall emergency vehicle ingress32. |
| **Medical Shelters** | \["amenity"="hospital"\] | Node / Way / Relation | Locates critical healthcare facilities to assess if they require emergency preemptive relocation or backup generator deployment. |
| **Emergency Hubs** | \["amenity"="police"\] | Node / Way / Relation | Maps command centers to ensure continuity of government operations during the crisis33. |

The Overpass API returns this requested data in a highly structured JSON format, containing precise geospatial coordinates and metadata tags27. The system utilizes a Python backend, leveraging libraries such as geopandas and overpy, to process this JSON payload30. The backend performs a spatial join (sjoin) between the infrastructure geometries retrieved from OSM and the deterministic flood polygons generated by the hydrodynamic PINNs30. This intersecting spatial analysis produces a definitive, localized list of critical assets that face imminent inundation, serving as the foundational vulnerability matrix for the cognitive orchestration layer.

## **Multimodal Reasoning and Agentic Orchestration (Gemini 3.7 Flash)**

The vast streams of data generated by the preceding tiers—geospatial SAR arrays, hydrodynamic surge predictions, and intersected JSON infrastructure node lists—must be synthesized into actionable, contextualized intelligence. This complex orchestration is executed by Gemini 3.7 Flash, Google’s most intelligent workhorse large language model tailored specifically for high-throughput agentic workflows, complex reasoning, code execution, and native structured data generation34.

### **Multimodal Vision and Contextual Synthesis**

Traditional automated data pipelines typically pass tabular data into rigid dashboards, requiring human analysts to interpret the meaning behind the numbers. Gemini 3.7 Flash fundamentally alters this paradigm by natively and simultaneously processing numeric tables, JSON infrastructure lists, and direct visual imagery within a massive 1-million token context window36. The platform exports the GEE inundation maps and PINN surge models as Base64-encoded Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs or standard Cloud Storage image URIs, passing them directly into Gemini’s context window alongside the meteorological telemetry and infrastructure matrices8.  
Operating as the central "Formation Controller AI," the Gemini 3.7 Flash agent assesses the spatial relationships visually and generates a holistic vulnerability narrative39. For example, by analyzing the intersected maps, the model can identify that an inundated arterial road not only blocks general civilian evacuation but specifically severs the only access route to a major coastal medical shelter. Recognizing this complex spatial dependency, the agent can formulate a nuanced advisory, prompting municipal authorities to initiate an immediate rerouting protocol or preemptive airborne evacuation for that specific facility.

### **Structured JSON Outputs and Deterministic Function Calling**

A critical requirement for an automated disaster management MVP is that the orchestrating AI does not output unstructured conversational text, but rather precise, machine-readable commands that trigger downstream microservices reliably. Gemini 3.7 Flash features native structured output enforcement. By utilizing the response\_schema parameter and defining the application/json MIME type, developers constrain the model's generation process directly, ensuring 100% valid JSON objects that conform perfectly to a predefined data schema40.  
The orchestrator utilizes function calling to interface autonomously with other systems42. If the predicted flood depth at a power substation exceeds 0.5 meters, the agent triggers a sequence of functions to format an early warning dispatch and execute an insurance smart contract. The model is constrained to output an exact JSON schema containing required keys such as event\_severity, affected\_infrastructure\_ids, recommended\_actions, and cap\_parameters, entirely eliminating parsing errors, hallucinations, and ensuring pipeline stability across the microservice architecture39.

## **Automated Dissemination and Network Resilience**

The transition from intelligent prediction to actionable warning requires strict adherence to international emergency communication standards, coupled with highly resilient physical transmission networks capable of surviving extreme weather.

### **The Common Alerting Protocol (CAP v1.2)**

When the Gemini 3.7 Flash agent determines that a meteorological threshold for anticipatory action has been breached, the platform automatically formats an advisory dispatch conforming precisely to the Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) v1.243. CAP is an international, non-proprietary standard defined by OASIS (Organization for the Advancement of Structured Information Standards) that ensures interoperability across all emergency warning systems globally, whether they are television broadcasts, highway signs, or mobile networks43.  
The platform generates CAP messages using JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data (JSON-LD) structures43. Generating CAP-compliant payloads enables seamless integration with governmental Cell Broadcast Services (CBS)46. Cell broadcasting is a specialized technology that sends instantaneous, geo-targeted popup notifications accompanied by distinctive emergency alarm tones to every mobile device currently connected to a cell tower within a specific geographic area47. Crucially, this bypasses the need to know individual phone numbers and remains immune to the network congestion that typically cripples standard SMS networks during a disaster47.  
The generated CAP JSON structure includes specific, machine-readable routing elements essential for automated parsing by downstream systems:

| CAP v1.2 Element | Data Type | Description and Platform Implementation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| identifier | String | A unique identifier for the alert, allowing systems to track updates or cancellations across the lifecycle of the event49. |
| msgType | Enum | Indicates whether the message is an Alert, Update, or Cancel50. |
| category | Enum | Set to Met (Meteorological) or Safety for infrastructural warnings50. |
| urgency | Enum | Defines the timeframe for action (e.g., Immediate, Expected, Future)50. |
| severity | Enum | Mapped to canonical tiers (Extreme, Severe, Moderate, Minor) ensuring downstream automations can react uniformly regardless of the hazard type49. |
| polygon | GeoJSON | Defines the exact geographic boundaries of the warning area (e.g., the PINN surge footprint), allowing cell towers to geo-target the broadcast43. |

### **Offline Mesh Networks (LoRaWAN)**

During catastrophic cyclones, cellular towers, fiber-optic lines, and regional power grids are routinely compromised, creating massive information blackouts precisely when early warnings and situational updates are most critical for survival52. To advance beyond current brittle communication architectures, the platform incorporates native support for Long Range Wide Area Network (LoRaWAN) mesh networking54.  
Solar-powered LoRa mesh nodes deployed in highly vulnerable coastal zones create decentralized, off-grid communication webs54. The platform's backend transmits the highly compressed CAP alerts through surviving internet gateways to edge nodes, which then propagate the warning across the LoRa mesh via low-frequency radio waves. This architecture ensures that even when the GSM network suffers a cascading failure, local disaster management authorities and community leaders receive the automated evacuation advisories, maintaining tactical command and control in the darkest hours of the crisis56.

## **Parametric Insurance and Liquidity Execution**

Perhaps the most significant bottleneck in post-disaster recovery is the extreme latency of financial liquidity. Traditional indemnity insurance requires claims adjusters to physically travel to disaster zones, manually inspect property damage, and navigate complex bureaucratic approval processes. This archaic methodology can delay payouts by months or even years, often leading to protracted legal disputes59. Anticipatory action frameworks have conclusively proven that providing vulnerable populations with unconditioned cash transfers 72 hours *before* a cyclone lands drastically improves food security and vastly improves psychosocial well-being, as citizens use the capital to secure livestock, stockpile food, and pay for evacuation transport4.  
The platform integrates parametric insurance protocols to automate and mathematically accelerate this liquidity transfer59. Unlike indemnity insurance, parametric insurance relies entirely on an objective parameter—in this case, the PINN-predicted storm surge height or the IMERG recorded wind speed—crossing a predetermined, contractual threshold59.

### **Smart Contracts and Decentralized Oracles**

To execute this liquidity transfer without any manual human intervention or bureaucratic delay, the insurance policies are encoded as self-executing smart contracts, utilizing the Solidity programming language on an Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM)-compatible blockchain59. However, blockchains are inherently isolated computational environments; they cannot natively make HTTP requests to external meteorological APIs to check if a cyclone has occurred63. This critical limitation, known in computer science as the "oracle problem," is resolved by integrating Chainlink Decentralized Oracle Networks (DONs)63.  
Chainlink DONs provide tamper-proof, cryptographic verification of off-chain data, bridging the gap between the platform's AI predictions and the blockchain's deterministic execution environment64. The architecture utilizes a Chainlink AggregatorV3Interface to pull the verified meteorological data on-chain63.  
The autonomous execution workflow proceeds as follows:

> 1. **Continuous Monitoring:** The platform continually assesses the cyclone parameters. If the Gemini 3.7 Flash agent confirms that a Category 3 equivalent surge is highly probable (e.g., \> 2.5 meters), it triggers a data push to the oracle network.  
> 2. **Oracle Verification:** Chainlink node operators independently query the platform's APIs, verify the meteorological metrics, and reach a decentralized consensus on the truth61.  
> 3. **Smart Contract Execution:** The decentralized oracle forwards the request to the oracle contract (which consumes approximately 935,806 gas per transaction) and triggers the settlement function within the indexed insurance contract66. The Solidity smart contract detects that the predefined parametric threshold has been breached and instantly executes, releasing the escrowed funds59.  
> 4. **Liquidity Distribution:** Through API integrations with prominent mobile money providers (such as bKash in Bangladesh), the released digital assets are instantly converted to local fiat currency and distributed directly to the mobile wallets of the pre-registered vulnerable demographics60. This provides immediate economic empowerment, allowing citizens to afford evacuation and secure their livelihoods days before the storm makes landfall60.

## **Advancements Over Current Technology**

The integration of these highly specialized systems yields several distinct, generational advancements over the current state-of-the-art in disaster management technology, directly answering the core challenge of the hackathon:

> 1. **From Reactive Bureaucracy to Anticipatory Automation:** Traditional workflows require human analysts to compile GIS data, write qualitative situation reports, wait for hierarchical bureaucratic approval, and manually initiate fund disbursements. This platform uses Gemini 3.7 Flash as an autonomous reasoning agent to compress a 48-hour human analytical workflow into seconds, triggering CAP alerts and smart contracts seamlessly without human friction36.  
> 2. **Replacing High-Latency Numerical Solvers with PINNs:** By utilizing Physics-Informed Neural Networks instead of traditional finite-element models like ADCIRC, the computational latency required to simulate storm surges and pluvial flooding is reduced by orders of magnitude. Crucially, by embedding equations into the loss function, the model achieves this speed without violating hydrodynamic laws of mass conservation16. This zero-shot speed allows for dynamic, iterative simulation of hundreds of potential storm tracks in real-time.  
> 3. **Multimodal Environmental Context:** Rather than forcing machine learning models to parse disjointed numerical tables, Gemini 3.7 Flash is utilized to visually ingest satellite inundation overlays. It interprets the complex spatial relationship between rising water and specific infrastructure nodes, mimicking human expert intuition at a massive scale37.  
> 4. **Cryptographic Trust in Humanitarian Liquidity:** Centralized aid distribution is notoriously hampered by logistical friction, political interference, or systemic corruption61. The use of Chainlink oracles and Solidity smart contracts ensures deterministic, tamper-proof payouts based entirely on objective, cryptographically verified data, enabling the UN OCHA anticipatory action philosophy to scale globally with absolute financial transparency1.  
> 5. **Off-Grid Communication Redundancy:** Moving beyond a dangerous dependency on fragile cellular infrastructure, the integration of LoRaWAN topologies ensures that the warning pipeline physically reaches ground-zero communities even when arterial power grids fail and telecommunication towers collapse56.

## **Requirements for the Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**

To physically build this architecture during a time-constrained hackathon environment, the following minimal viable components, frameworks, and access keys must be provisioned and integrated:

### **1\. Cloud and AI Infrastructure (Google Cloud Platform)**

* **Vertex AI:** Required for deploying the PINN/DUALFloodGNN inference endpoints and hosting the Gemini 3.7 Flash agent. Developers will interact with this using the google-genai Python SDK, ensuring that response\_schema constraints are strictly enforced for all JSON outputs40.  
* **Cloud Run:** Necessary to host decoupled microservices (e.g., the API listener for Overpass requests, the CAP generator webhook, and the frontend dashboard) in containerized Docker environments. This allows for automatic, serverless scaling upon heavy request loads during an emergency6.  
* **Google Cloud Storage (GCS):** Acts as the central data lake for temporarily caching Sentinel-1 GeoTIFF outputs and making them available as URIs for Gemini's multimodal prompt consumption, bypassing local memory limits8.

### **2\. Geospatial Data and APIs**

* **Google Earth Engine API:** A registered GEE Service Account is mandatory to programmatically call Sentinel-1 GRD, GPM IMERG, and Copernicus DEM datasets. The backend will utilize the Python ee and geemap libraries to execute the Otsu thresholding and focal median smoothing algorithms8.  
* **OpenStreetMap Overpass API:** Access to public Overpass API endpoints (e.g., overpass-api.de) or a locally hosted instance is required for executing QL spatial queries. The overpy and geopandas Python wrappers will handle the extraction and spatial joining of the JSON responses27.

### **3\. Blockchain and Smart Contract Tooling**

* **Hardhat / Foundry:** Local development environments required for writing, compiling, testing, and deploying the Solidity parametric insurance contract (IndexedInsurance.sol)64.  
* **Chainlink Functions / Decentralized Oracles:** Required for securely bridging the off-chain weather API data into the on-chain smart contract, ensuring the parametric trigger is cryptographically verified64.  
* **EVM Testnet Access:** An RPC endpoint (provided by services like Alchemy or Infura) and a funded developer wallet (e.g., loaded with Sepolia ETH) are necessary to execute the smart contract on an Ethereum testnet for the live MVP demonstration.

### **4\. Alerting Standards and Front-End Visualization**

* **CAP Generation Module:** A Python microservice utilizing standard JSON libraries to map the Gemini agent's structured output strictly to the OASIS CAP v1.2 standard format, ensuring compatibility with mock Cell Broadcast Systems43.  
* **Interactive Dashboarding:** A lightweight frontend framework (e.g., Streamlit, or a React application utilizing mapping libraries like Mapbox GL JS or Deck.gl) to visualize the storm surge polygons, highlight the intersected critical infrastructure nodes, and display real-time logs of the Gemini agent's reasoning process and JSON dispatches.

## **Conclusion**

The convergence of artificial intelligence, high-performance geospatial computing, and decentralized finance presents an unprecedented opportunity to fundamentally redefine disaster response in the Asia-Pacific region. By transitioning operations from post-landfall reaction to pre-landfall action, the staggering socioeconomic cost of extreme weather is heavily mitigated. This architectural blueprint demonstrates that by utilizing Gemini 3.7 Flash as a centralized multimodal orchestrator, Google Earth Engine for planetary-scale environmental telemetry, Physics-Informed Neural Networks for low-latency hydrodynamic simulation, and Chainlink-enabled smart contracts for instantaneous liquidity, vulnerable communities in the Bay of Bengal can be mathematically and financially hardened against escalating climatic threats. Implementing this Minimum Viable Product offers a scalable, transparent, and highly resilient shield, ultimately saving lives, preserving critical infrastructure, and securing livelihoods against the ravages of extreme coastal weather.
